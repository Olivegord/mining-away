# Independent visual quality gate

## Authority and purpose

The owner's October 27 request makes this a completion requirement: a separate, severe visual reviewer must reject generic, AI-looking work and refuse to call the game finished until it looks good. Read `OWNER_REQUIREMENTS.md` first, then `ORIGINAL_FEEL_PARITY.md`. This gate supplements them; it cannot relax gameplay, preservation, authority, or persistence requirements. The latest direction is an intentional **parts-built retro Roblox map**, smaller interactive chance-based card UI, and excellent beam feedback, with Bee Swarm Simulator as an exemplar rather than an asset source.

“AI-looking” is a criticism of observable design, not a claim about how an asset was produced. Evaluate composition, coherence, specificity, interactions and craft. Do not pretend to detect authorship from pixels. More objects, more glow, more gradients and more rounded panels do not establish quality.

## Required independent reviewer assignment

Give a separate subagent the following instructions verbatim at implementation start and again with the final evidence bundle:

> You are Mining Away's independent art director and hostile quality reviewer. Your job is to protect the player from generic, lazy, AI-looking output. You are not the implementer's advocate. Do not reward effort, test counts, screenshots from old builds, impressive prose, or claims that the code ought to look good. Inspect actual current Studio screenshots and motion evidence before any visual PASS. Be blunt and specific: identify ugly proportions, empty dead space, repeated prefabs, meaningless decoration, muddy lighting, floating objects, garish glow, giant interfaces, bad type hierarchy and weak feedback. Reject them. Compare ordinary player-camera views, not only a flattering overview. Preserve the original drones, crystals, world identity and segmented pyramid turret. Demand a coherent parts-built retro world, immediate and readable gameplay, compact interactive three-choice card reveals, and well-attached restrained beams. No minimum score or calendar deadline can override a blocker. For each rejection give the precise capture/time/location, visible defect, player consequence, concrete correction and evidence needed for recheck. Require the implementer to fix it and return fresh evidence. Re-review every changed area and the surrounding scene. You must return REJECT, UNVERIFIED, or PASS; do not mark the goal done unless current visual evidence earns PASS and required gameplay/multiplayer gates pass separately. If Studio is unavailable, return UNVERIFIED with exact missing evidence, while the implementer continues all productive work. Never fabricate a playtest or imply that a source inspection proves visual quality.

The reviewer must not be the agent that authored the work being judged. The implementing agent cannot self-award or waive the verdict. Record reviewer identity/task, date, exact commit, evidence paths and verdict in the review record. A visual PASS is necessary, not sufficient, for overall completion.

## Evidence needed for a verdict

### Owner's explicit hard rejects: sloppy execution

The owner clarified that weak art direction is only part of the problem: **cut-off beams, ugly particles, bad animation and floating trees are automatic rejection conditions.** Treat these as defects to find actively, not optional polish. Inspect the final built game in motion from normal player, close, oblique and phone-sized views; a flattering still cannot clear them.

- **Cut-off or detached beams:** reject visibly truncated/clipped segments, gaps between beam layers, emitter origins detached from moving drones, endpoints detached from moving/shrinking crystals, and beams disappearing incorrectly with camera angle or distance. Check growth, every fracture stage, target changes, recall and death. Intentional occlusion by solid geometry must remain visually coherent; effects must not appear to terminate arbitrarily in empty space.
- **Ugly particles:** reject visible square texture cards or harsh sprite borders, muddy/cheap sprites, excessive density, popping lifetimes, clipping through the drone/crystal, and emitters originating inside or through unrelated surfaces. Reject particles that overwhelm the beam core, hide the target or remain after the source is gone. Check normal-speed motion and maximum representative simultaneous effects, not a single staged emitter.
- **Bad animations:** reject snapping, jitter, visible frame stepping, unintended foot sliding where characters animate, intersecting or clipping parts, hovering fragments, poor ease-in/out and abrupt or mismatched state transitions. Watch approach-to-mine, warmup-to-contact, fracture-to-break, combat-to-death and reprint-to-follow transitions repeatedly. Correct mathematics does not excuse ugly observed motion.
- **Floating or buried scenery:** reject floating trees, disconnected trunks/roots, buried canopies or props, hovering path pieces and bad terrain/part joins. Check the contact point of every placed tree and major prop, especially slopes, steps and field borders, from side and player-height views. Shadows cannot disguise a gap. Ground intentionally elevated objects with visible supports or a clear authored explanation.
- **No favorable-angle exemption:** sweep the ordinary camera around each affected object/effect, move through the scene, vary zoom within normal gameplay, and inspect the phone viewport. A defect visible during plausible play is a defect even if the overview screenshot looks attractive. Provide fresh motion evidence after every repair.

Record the exact built commit, source cleanliness and build SHA from `build/revision.json`. Evidence must identify that build; old screenshots are comparison material only. Capture at normal play scale and record viewport/device. Include unedited gameplay footage or a timestamped sequence of actual Studio screenshots sufficient to evaluate timing and interaction. Generated illustrations, browser mockups and outside-engine maps are never substitutes.

1. **World:** player-height spawn/home, safe field, connecting route, guarded field and reverse return view; one overview for spatial context. Show stations in use and traversal without flight/free-camera shortcuts. Compare old/new views at comparable framing.
2. **Card UI:** idle HUD, purchase/reveal, all three choices, hovered/focused card detail, press/selection feedback, applied result, close/reopen and insufficient currency. Show a desktop and phone-sized viewport. Record actual reveal duration and interaction behavior; explain any omitted input method.
3. **Mining:** an uninterrupted normal-speed clip from follow through a 30+ stud assignment, settled overhead warmup/contact, all three fracture changes, break, regrowth and recall. Include a close view of the emitter and impact point plus ordinary gameplay distance. Include multiple beams without staging away clutter.
4. **Combat:** actual pyramid telegraph/fire, damage, drone destruction, free reprint, and a defeated turret. Show effect cleanup on recall, target destruction and scene transitions.
5. **Multiplayer, separately:** real Studio server with at least two clients showing both fleets, beams, stages and combat; add a late join while mining/growth is active. Report observed smoothness and replicated state on each client. A single-client clip or API double cannot pass this gate.
6. **Gameplay/pacing, separately:** a measured fresh-profile early loop that includes first pack and an actual card decision, plus a recorded route/combat choice. Distinguish elapsed real playtime from simulation. Verify progression/persistence under the separate owner acceptance plan; visual review cannot certify storage safety.

## Review procedure and completion rule

- First review: original behavior/identity parity and first playable loop. Identify regressions before adding cosmetic layers.
- Second review: art, UI interaction, effects, pacing and actual multiplayer. Inspect the full evidence bundle, especially ordinary player views.
- The reviewer returns a table of concrete defects with severity (`blocker`, `major`, `minor`), evidence and corrective action. Any blocker or major defect prevents PASS. Minor defects may be documented only if they do not undermine the owner's requested quality; do not relabel a major defect to close the task.
- After fixes, provide fresh evidence and obtain another independent verdict. No automatic PASS after a fixed number of iterations. The reviewer must explain why the final composition, UI and effects are coherent and convincing; silence is not approval.
- Without Studio, complete implementation, research, simulations, reproducible build and review PR, but label visual acceptance **UNVERIFIED** and leave the goal incomplete. State exactly which tests remain and what integration prevented them. Continue available productive work rather than waiting idle or inventing evidence.
- Final reporting must separate code/build checks, modeled pacing, actual single-client Studio observation, actual multiplayer and independent visual verdict. Do not publish Roblox or create a separate experience.
