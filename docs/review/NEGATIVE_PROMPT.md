# Mining Away visual rejection guide

This is an actionable brief for the next candidate, not a style classifier or proof of AI authorship. A person can make any of these mistakes, and an AI-assisted build can avoid them. Judge the artifact and the player's view, not how it was made. **Do not call the goal visually complete without the independent art director inspecting actual final-build Roblox renders and movement.** A mock, static source inspection, generated screenshot of an imagined build, clean code or a passing test suite cannot satisfy that gate.

## Direction to preserve

An original retro Roblox parts-built alien mining world: compact connected fields with crisp, purposeful slab/brick/stud details; a depot, Converter, card machine, original mining drones and crystals; original segmented `construct` pyramid as an active guard. Preserve source assets and keep the original recognizable. Protect the original eased/capped follow/travel, level yaw and overhead station; random 50–100 HP crystal variation, roughly two-second growth, 75/50/25% stages and final break. The server owns positions relevant to gameplay, rewards, combat, card rolls, saved offers, target IDs and prestige. A client effect may only reveal or decorate an already-authoritative state.

Use Bee Swarm Simulator as a reference for a comprehensible sequence of nearby fields, memorable landmarks, physical stations and a growing workforce; do not copy its characters, flowers, artwork, UI or branding. The creator's [official game listing](https://www.roblox.com/games/1537690962/Bee-Swarm-Simulator) describes collection, conversion, a growing swarm, exploring farther and threats. It establishes the broad loop, not that the current Mining Away build looks or plays like it.

## Explicit negative prompt paired with authored alternatives

| Reject this in the candidate | Create this instead for Mining Away | Inspect in the final build |
|---|---|---|
| Interchangeable floating low-poly islands; faceted terrain used as generic filler; a detached test map | A connected parts-built ground route: a legible depot, nearby starter fields, a visible next guard and a later destination, with deliberate ledges/steps and no unrelated overlay | Spawn-to-first-field walk and a wide elevated view; can a player identify the next place without a map? |
| Huge empty platforms or an undifferentiated flat rectangle used for every zone | A human-scale clearing with intentional footprint, clear work area and meaningful perimeter; change field silhouettes, edges and landmark placement to support the actual route | Stand inside each field at normal camera distance; compare how each entry/exit reads |
| Repeated cone forests or the same tree evenly rotated in every direction | Two or three related, parts-built tree silhouettes that frame paths and protect sightlines; place clusters intentionally, retain open mining space and ground every trunk | Walk depot → safe field → guarded field; check whether repetition becomes conspicuous or blocks the drone/rock |
| Random crystals, rocks, crates, pipes or neon props used only to fill voids | Every visible object has a job: resource, obstacle, route edge, station or one distinctive landmark; server-random resource placement remains organic within safe field bounds | Look for isolated decoration unrelated to navigation or play; verify nodes don't overlap the turret or exit |
| Ungrounded trees, hovering feet/props, buried stations, impossible slopes, gaps between path pieces, seams that shimmer | Seat every visible object on its supporting surface; align walkable pieces and make grade/edges intentional; retain the original world rather than covering it | Low-angle ground-level sweep, traverse each connection both ways, and inspect station bases/field edges |
| A pile of unrelated visual themes or incompatible palettes | One base palette of restrained solid materials plus a small field-specific mineral accent; repeat shape and construction vocabulary at the depot, paths and guarded areas | Compare the spawn, starter field and guarded field in one route; remove styles that have no relationship to the rest |
| Giant HUDs, dashboard panels, persistent debug labels and billboard spam hiding the world | A compact credits/cargo/fleet/objective HUD; show station controls only nearby, one relevant guard health readout during combat, and a choice interface only when the offer exists | Check normal play and combat at desktop and small touch viewports; drone, target, guard and route remain visible |
| Stock gradients, rainbow card stacks, oversized glowing reward cards, confetti that blocks all three options | Small tactile three-card choices with a clear title, exact benefit/drawback, scope/target and **This run**; one restrained mineral-colored accent and a short skip-friendly reveal of already saved choices | Open a paid offer; compare all three at once, then close/reopen/rejoin and verify identical choices/target and no new charge |
| Rarity colors, moving reels or near-misses that imply odds the actual selection does not use | Use rarity only if it reflects genuine server-defined odds and explain the category; otherwise use neutral category/type cues. Never fake a near miss or imply a reroll | Compare displayed labels to actual server catalog/roll; watch reopens and repeats; no paid Robux RNG |
| Mismatched stock icons, unrelated typeface families, unlabeled colors, microscopic or clipped effect copy | A small consistent icon/shape set, readable text for the named drone or color, and one typography hierarchy. Short effect labels retain their numerical meaning; color is never the sole distinction | Read each card on phone and desktop, including the longest target/effect and larger text settings; test hover, focus, keyboard and touch |
| Arbitrary neon trim, global bloom, rainbow light, a glow on every object | Mostly solid, readable parts with restrained emissive accents only where they explain interaction: emitter, crystal impact and sentry charge; preserve field colors/materials | Compare lowest and highest graphics quality in a busy field. The crystal and beam core remain visible without washed-out backgrounds |
| Broken, detached, sideways, cutoff, opaque or jittering mining beams; halos wider than the target | Anchor beam origin to the rendered original drone emitter and end at the current visible rock surface; retain a slender bright core with a softer, quieter halo, intentional startup and short hide, no pitched body | Record a normal assignment, close approach, mining through all stages, target break, recall, death and reprint; compare both viewers and a late joiner |
| Opaque square particles, unrelated spark colors, permanent uncontrolled emitters, visual noise hiding the reward | Small bounded brief chips/sparks at the actual impact or break; use a consistent sprite and material-aware color, cap concurrent effects and clean up on despawn; reduce motion where requested | Watch one hit and six simultaneous beams on low/high graphics; check for runaway particles, stale endpoints and completely hidden rocks |
| Snapping/jittering flight, clipped drone/rock, disconnected shard transitions, body nose-diving, animation that masks server state | One smooth elapsed-time-correct presentation of server intent, level yaw, original eased/capped approach; drone settles above its assigned rock; visible sequential fractures end in one clear break | Compare original and candidate moving footage at 30/60/144 FPS and matching camera distance; include busy multiplayer and close-up stage/break footage |
| A loud long reveal, fake randomness, a screen-filling flash or a forced wait after an offer already exists | A brief, skippable tactile anticipation/shutter followed by all three *previously persisted* choices; focus/hover/tap/pressed feedback, immediate access on reopen, server-confirmed selection | Slow/failed network, tap twice, close/reopen and reduced-motion checks; no extra charges or alternate offer can appear |

These are rejection criteria to test, not a claim that each currently occurs. A recognizable flat football field or deliberate cone tree is not intrinsically a defect; the question is whether the result serves this specific mining game.

## Evidence from public AI-assisted game work

- In an [AI-agent-created Roblox mining tycoon account](https://medium.com/@andy.a.g/i-built-a-roblox-game-using-only-ai-agents-heres-what-happened-ed57b553facc), the author reports floating/overlapping portal pads, unreadable billboard text, a later zone that repeated the first flat grid, screen-size overlap and respawned blocks turning white after a hit effect. These are the author's reported playtest observations, not a firsthand inspection of that build here, and do not establish that most AI-made games share them. Mining Away implication: validate physical support, visual distinctions, UI at multiple sizes, and true mineral color after every break/regrowth.
- [Paralov's first Studs.gg soccer demo](https://paralov.com/blog/first-demo-soccer/) is explicitly described by its creator as generated from a single prompt and rough. Its own screenshot caption identifies a flat, enclosed green pitch, ball, player and goal. A football pitch can properly be flat; the relevant inference for Mining Away is that a bare undifferentiated rectangle does not by itself communicate several fields or a memorable mining route. The article supplies the demo screenshot and video; visual quality cannot be established from its caption alone.
- A [Roblox festival game's developer](https://note.com/westury/n/n918a3e6cdcad?hl=en) describes using generated reference art and 3D models, then manually arranging the environment. They report that reducing model geometry distorted written stall text and that painted patterns became unwanted bumps. That is a reported workflow-specific failure, not a general verdict on a tool. Mining Away implication: favor authored simple parts for shape, use readable native text separately, and inspect any reused asset at real game scale.
- [Roblox's own Build evaluation report](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/10/how-roblox-evaluates-build-game-creation) treats visual/experiential quality, playtest behavior and static engineering as separate signals, including captured gameplay at different viewport sizes. Its examples include uncluttered/legible UI, and it warns that code structure alone does not establish a playable or polished result. This supports the independent capture gate; it does not rate this project.

This research used source text and search-index image descriptions. In this cloud environment the retrieved reference-image URLs did not provide an inspectable image payload, and a YouTube fetch was throttled. No original exemplar frame or final candidate frame was directly inspected here. The above sources are examples, not statistics or proof of what “most” generated games look like.

## Owner-selected card reference: inspected official screenshots

The official [Celestial Pack screenshot](https://www.playbalatro.com/press-kit/Screenshots/Celestial%20Pack.png) was inspected as actual pixels (1920 × 1080). Its settled choice state places three separated portrait cards (Mars, Mercury, Uranus) in a lower-center row, roughly 160 × 240 pixels each. A compact Mercury details panel floats above the center card, and the pack title and one Skip control sit immediately below the row. The separate [Arcana Packs screenshot](https://www.playbalatro.com/press-kit/Screenshots/Arcana%20Packs.png) shows a sheet of eight illustrated wrappers, not a three-card choice. These screenshots show a captured state, not animation timing or a complete interaction sequence.

For Mining Away, adapt the visual hierarchy: three compact distinct choices, one focused explanation close to its choice, a small action below, and room to see the world. Hover, gamepad focus and touch may lift or highlight a choice gently; test them in motion rather than claiming a screenshot proves the behavior. The explanation must show the exact effect, named target where applicable and run-only scope; an offer must reopen unchanged. Author original drone and mineral imagery; do not reuse the source art, branding, typography, game HUD or wrapper designs. These are references, not project assets.

## Prompt guidance and attribution

- [Anthropic's Opus 5.5 prompting guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5) observes that a generic instruction to avoid an AI look can swap one default frontend style for another, and recommends naming specific patterns and iterating on what actually appeared. That is Anthropic's guidance for its own model and frontend work, not a validated rule about Roblox or evidence that visual style identifies authorship. Here the table above adds specific observable failures and desired replacements.
- [Roblox's Assistant prompt guide](https://create.roblox.com/docs/assistant/prompt-engineering) recommends exact instance names, specific operations and distinguishing runtime from edit-time changes. Apply that to `construct`, `bottemseg`, `eye`, original drone emitters and the real source crystal: preserve their existing identities and request tested runtime behavior, not an anonymous replacement.
- [PromptBlox's own guide](https://promptblox.ai/docs/prompt-guide) publishes a reusable build-prompt template covering player actions, ownership/shared state, persistence, controls, visual direction and must-haves. It calls for trying the result in Studio and says its guide/preview does not guarantee a working feature. This is a vendor-published example template; no independent evidence found that it is the most-used or a popular community standard. Its examples are leads, not permission to copy or execute third-party scripts.
- [Roblox's beam guide](https://create.roblox.com/docs/effects/beams) documents attachments, camera-facing behavior and low/high graphics testing; the [Beam reference](https://create.roblox.com/docs/reference/engine/classes/Beam) describes `ZOffset` for layered beams and the relationship between sequence keypoints and segments. [Performance guidance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/improve) cautions that decals and particles do not batch well and emitter property changes can be expensive. [Accessibility guidance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/accessibility) covers readable contrast, player text size and reduced motion. Apply and test these rules, rather than substituting more effects for actual clarity.

## Independent veto: evidence required for completion

The art director has authority to return **FAIL** for an observed defect or **NOT VERIFIED** for missing evidence. Neither outcome may be converted to approval by the implementation agent. Approval is allowed only after inspecting evidence from the **exact final candidate commit/build** and explicitly re-reviewing repaired issues.

Capture actual Roblox Studio gameplay, with an unchanged original comparison where possible:

1. Ground-level spawn and a continuous walk from depot past the first safe field to the first guarded field, plus a higher overview that shows the route and original landmarks. Inspect tree/station footings, path continuity, field identity and sightlines.
2. One normal drone: follow, travel, settle directly above a rock, visible growth and all three fracture stages, break, a fresh crystal regrowth and recall. Record movement at 30/60/144 FPS where feasible. Inspect emitter-to-moving-surface attachment and cleanup on target loss/death.
3. The saved three-card offer opening, all options readable, focus/hover/pressed feedback, selection, close/reopen and same-offer rejoin. Capture desktop and narrow touch viewport, including increased text and reduced motion where supported.
4. One full pyramid charge, frozen-aim telegraph, dodge, firing, defeat, drone destruction and free reconstruction. Show all stages in motion, without a giant health panel or a beam hiding the target.
5. A busy field with multiple drones and a second client/late joiner. Compare both observers' gameplay state, beam origin/endpoint, particles and reprint visuals; test low and high graphics settings and a representative mobile/desktop viewport.

For every defect, report scene, camera/time, exact failure, severity and specific repair; fix and re-capture the same case from the new final candidate. A visual PASS requires the reviewer to assess all applicable captured states. If Studio/engine rendering is unavailable or the final artifact cannot be viewed, record **NOT VERIFIED**, continue useful implementation, and deliver the review build honestly without claiming the full visual goal is done.
