## Construction decisions

- The map retains the original field centers, ore budgets, route plan, spawn, Converter, drone/crystal assets and exact segmented construct. Existing original objects and `assets/mining-away-base.rbxl` are preserved. Runtime generated props extend the same game; there is no elevated second world.
- `MapLayout` derives 26 × 26 physical square caps and stepped solid Part terraces using the *minimum* of each former slope's four clearance vertices, quantized down to half-stud increments. This keeps the new flat cap at or below the previously checked route/field clearance envelope. The current default plan has 676 caps, 285 raised cells, nine original routed trails, and no generated native meadow cells. `NativeMeadow` and `Facets` remain as source/reference but are not invoked for this map. `TopSurface.Studs` supplies the retro construction language; actual appearance remains an engine check.
- A subdued dark baseplate and low segmental retaining edge define the playable landscape rather than an infinite mint plane. The palette is a coherent green/stone terrain with mineral-specific field and tree accents, pale route material, dark machinery and restrained mint/amber machinery indicators.
- Full nonblocking rectangular field rims identify where each mining area starts. Four short physical depot links join the existing starter-route endpoints to the apron while clearing all three stations. The overhead-beacon gantry gives the depot one intentional skyline silhouette. Ruby mine portal, upper aqueduct and the original construct remain distinctive district landmarks rather than new copied Bee Swarm props.
- Removing thin dark shoulders from every curved route chord targets the actual comb-tooth artifact visible in the historical overview. Same-color joint caps overlap the chords, sit just below their top surfaces, and have solid vertical `JointEarth` support when raised. The underlying `Trail` objects retain the source route metadata and support the existing walk tests; four new depot links are separate one-segment routes.
- Forty-one old planned trees reduce to **34** in the default plan because trees with a more than 4.5-stud footprint height spread are rejected; ten remain in Ruby. Each tree has a flat part footing whose top equals the tallest sampled terrain surface under its footprint and whose bottom reaches the lowest. Trunks and roots start at that footing's top without pitch/roll. Original tree source objects are retained and grounded from their rotated part-corner bounds. Sparse clusters replace the old four-per-tree decorative scatter.
- The mine has continuous physical foundations and separate portal footings; lamp cables join lights to the crossbeam. The elevated overlook has four actual support posts. The preserved pyramid landmark rests on a flat plinth above the highest terrain under its footprint, with supporting bedrock. Gate piers, pool and small natural props use sampled footings. Source code avoids hiding a floating buttress on an enormous improvised pedestal; steep-footprint decorative blocks are skipped.
